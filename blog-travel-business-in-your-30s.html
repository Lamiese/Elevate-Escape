Building a Travel Business in Your 30s: Niches Worth Considering

Your 30s are a genuinely great time to start a travel business: you've got a real network, more disposable income among your friends, and a clearer sense of what kind of trips actually excite you. Here are some niches worth building around.

Why your 30s are a good moment to start this

By your 30s, you've usually got a wide circle of friends, colleagues, and family who are travelling more than ever, often with more money to spend on it than they had in their 20s. Hen parties, milestone birthdays, first family holidays with young kids, big anniversary trips: this is the decade when people start actually splashing out on travel rather than backpacking on a shoestring. That makes it a genuinely strong starting point for building a travel business, because the clients are already around you.

The trick is picking a niche that plays to what you already love and know, rather than trying to be everything to everyone. Here are a few worth thinking about.

Adventure and escape trips

If you're someone who loves getting outdoors, hiking, diving, road-tripping, or chasing a bit of adrenaline, this is a brilliant niche to build around. Adventure travellers tend to be repeat clients who book often and refer their equally adventurous friends. You don't need to have done everything yourself, but genuine enthusiasm for this kind of travel comes through and builds trust fast.

Female-only trips

Women-only travel has grown hugely, and for good reason: a lot of women want to travel with other women, whether that's a girls' trip, a wellness retreat, or a solo woman wanting the reassurance of a group. If this speaks to you, it's a niche with a genuinely engaged, loyal client base and a strong sense of community built in from the start.

Group travel

Group bookings, whether that's a hen do, a big milestone birthday, a friend group's annual trip, or a family reunion, are one of the most natural places to start, because you're often already the one organising these trips informally among your friends. Turning that into a proper business means you get paid for something you're probably already doing for free.

The common thread: the strongest niches are the ones you'd be excited to book yourself. Clients can tell the difference between someone reciting a brochure and someone who genuinely loves the kind of trip they're selling.

Starting small and building from there

You don't need to pick just one niche forever, and you don't need a huge following to start. Most people begin with the trips their own friends and family are already planning, get a few genuinely great experiences under their belt, and let word of mouth do the rest. The niche just helps you know where to focus your energy and who to speak to first.
