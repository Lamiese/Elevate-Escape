How to Build a Travel Business Without a Big Network

No huge following, no big extended family to book holidays for, barely active on social media, but you still want to do this? Good news: none of that is actually a dealbreaker. Here's how to start from scratch.

Let's address the elephant in the room

A lot of advice about starting a travel business assumes you've got hundreds of Instagram followers or a sprawling family who'll book every holiday through you. If that's not your situation, you might be wondering if this is even worth trying. It is, and honestly, plenty of successful advisors started with a genuinely small circle. Your first few clients don't need to number in the hundreds. They need to number in the low single figures, done really well.

You don't need social media to start

It helps, eventually, but it's not where day one has to begin. Word of mouth from real conversations still works, arguably better than a cold Instagram post ever will. Tell the people you do see, at work, at the gym, at your local coffee shop, that you're doing this now. One good conversation with someone actually planning a trip is worth more than a hundred passive followers.

Look sideways, not just at family

If your family isn't a big travelling bunch, that's fine, because your client base was never meant to be limited to relatives. Colleagues, old school friends you're still in touch with, people from a hobby group or a gym class, neighbours: these are all real people planning real trips who have no idea you can help them, until you tell them.

A useful reframe: you're not trying to convert your entire existing circle into clients overnight. You're trying to find the two or three people, in any part of your life, who are already thinking about booking a trip right now.

Let your first bookings do the talking

Once you've booked a handful of trips well, on time, without drama, at a good price, those clients become your best source of new ones. A genuinely happy client tells other people without being asked. That's how a network gets built from nothing: not by starting with one, but by earning one, one good booking at a time.

Patience is the real requirement

This route is slower to start than if you already had a big built-in audience, and that's worth being honest about. But slow and steady is still a real business. The people who stick with it, even when the first few months feel quiet, are the ones who end up with something solid a year or two in.
